When I first got to Bali, I felt like a square peg in a round hole. Unseen, out of place, faintly wrong. I know this nerve. I've met it before. And I know its only balm is time, which is annoying, because time is the one thing I can't optimize.

So I did what I do with everything: I built a routine and clung to it like a life raft. Beach walk, 6pm, every day. An anchor. Very healthy. Very stable.

Until the anchor tried to kill me.

The plan was the usual walk. The execution was two-plus kilometres in the direction with the fewest people, because apparently my instinct for safety is "go where no one could possibly help you." Denim shorts, black strapless top, hair gone full feral in the humidity, big bulky headphones on. I tried to lose myself in the music. I succeeded. Completely. Literally. I stopped tracking the direction because ABBA was on and priorities are priorities.

6pm became 7. The moody sky became a raging storm.

Fine - not a storm. A torrential downpour. But you know how Mumbai girls are. Rain? Meh. Drizzle. We don't run.

Only it wasn't drizzle. Five minutes in, soaked through to the bone. I finally look up and take stock of my surroundings, and my surroundings are: nothing. Every shop shuttered. No clubs, no hotels, no people. Just me, standing in what appeared to be the aftermath of a minor flood, dressed for a rooftop bar.

Breathe in. Breathe out. ABBA is still here. That's something.

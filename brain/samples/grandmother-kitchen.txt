My grandmother's kitchen always smelled of cardamom and something burning slightly at the edges. I used to sit at the table while she worked, my feet not reaching the floor. The afternoon light came through the window in long yellow bars, and dust turned slowly in it. She never spoke much. She would press a warm cup of tea into my hands and I would hold it, not drinking, just letting the heat move into my fingers. There was an old photograph on the shelf, faded almost to nothing, of a woman I never met. Years later I still think of that room. I think of her hands. I think of the quiet.
